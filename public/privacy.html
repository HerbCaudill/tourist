Privacy

Updated 9 September 2026

Tourist uses a location and your questions to research nearby stories. The live research features described here are being introduced into the prototype. A demonstration using fixed sample stories does not submit a live research request.

Location and Google Maps

Your browser asks permission before providing your device's location. You can refuse and enter a street or landmark instead, or revoke permission in your browser settings.

For live discovery, Tourist sends coordinates and their reported accuracy to its backend. Google receives the typed place query when resolving a location, coordinates and a search radius when finding nearby places, and the map center and markers when creating a map image. These requests go through Tourist's backend.

Tourist's Google Maps features are subject to the Google Privacy Policy, incorporated here for those features. Google processes data under that policy. Map images retain Google's attribution. Provider place names are temporary research clues; the stories and their labels are researched from linked sources.

Research and questions

Tourist's backend runs on Vercel. A research service on Cloudflare sends the research location, nearby place clues and relevant context to OpenAI's Codex service for research. A follow-up question also includes a bounded portion of the conversation and relevant stories. Research can involve web searches and reading external source pages.

Research prompts and results are encrypted in Tourist's Cloudflare job storage so work can continue if your phone disconnects. Jobs become unavailable 24 hours after creation and are scheduled for deletion then. Storage or delivery failures can delay physical deletion. This period describes Tourist's job storage; service providers' own data policies also apply.

The app uses encrypted, expiring recovery tickets to resume research. Its API sends location and conversation data in request bodies rather than page addresses. Tourist's application code does not log research payloads or raw provider errors. Hosting and service providers can process technical request information needed to operate their services.

Data in your browser

Tourist caches app files for reopening. Local reading history and a clear-history control are being added. When that feature is available, recent discoveries and conversations will be saved in this browser for offline reading, with bounded retention. Google map images and raw nearby-place results will not be included in that archive.

You can remove Tourist's local data through your browser's clear-site-data controls. This does not erase research already sent to service providers or cancel a submitted job. Research job expiry is separate from browser storage.

Links to sources

Opening a source link takes you to another website, which has its own privacy practices. Tourist does not control that site's content or data collection.
