Tourist · design directions

Six directions for the nearby-stories app in plan 001, all showing the same fictional moment: Herb on Candlemaker Row, Edinburgh, at 16:42 with three stories within 200 m. Compare shape, not content.

Why: A conventional card feed with a persistent ask bar: previews stack top to bottom, strongest first, and every screen ends in the same input.

Tradeoff: It looks like every other app, and the third card is already below the fold on a phone.

Why: One story per screen at magazine scale, so the preview teaches without a tap and the distance reads like a headline; swipe sideways for the next.

Tradeoff: You cannot see the three previews together, so choosing between them means paging through all of them.

Why: Distance and direction come first: a compass plot shows where each story sits relative to you before the list explains what it is.

Tradeoff: It promises spatial precision the plan explicitly says not to imply, and the plot eats half the screen before any prose appears.

Why: The whole app is one conversation: discoveries arrive as messages, stories expand in place, and asking a question is the same gesture as everything else.

Tradeoff: Reading a long story inside a chat bubble is cramped, and old discoveries scroll away into the transcript.

Why: A dense monospace list that fits all three stories, their tags, and the prompt on one screen with no chrome, for reading while walking.

Tradeoff: The plain-text look makes folklore and documented fact feel equally flat, and it will not charm anyone who is not Herb.

Why: Off-plan: a lock-screen notification and a home-screen widget deliver the top preview without opening anything, which is the purest form of “teach without a tap”.

Tradeoff: It depends on proactive location and notifications, which the plan defers, and a PWA cannot ship a real iOS widget today.
